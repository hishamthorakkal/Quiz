# SECTION C — DETAILED EXPLANATIONS (Selected High-Yield Questions)

---

**Q1 — B (Chest compressions)**
After 30 seconds of effective PPV, if HR <60/min, initiate chest compressions at 3:1 ratio (90 compressions:30 breaths per minute). Per NRP 2020, intubation should accompany compressions but is not the "next step" if PPV is already working — the threshold for compressions is HR <60 after effective PPV for 30 seconds. Epinephrine (C) is only given if HR remains <60 after adequate compressions + ventilation.
*Exam Pearl: HR <60 after 30 sec effective PPV → Compressions. HR <60 after compressions + ventilation → Epinephrine.*

---

**Q2 — D (Mask adjustment)**
MR SOPA mnemonic for failed PPV: **M**ask adjustment → **R**eposition airway → **S**uction → **O**pen mouth → **P**ressure increase → **A**irway alternative. Mask adjustment is ALWAYS first.
*Trap: Many choose "reposition" first, but mask leak is far more common than airway malposition.*

---

**Q3 — C (Intubate + surfactant)**
A 28-week infant on CPAP 6 with FiO2 0.35 at 20 minutes with RDS on CXR requires rescue surfactant (INSURE or LISA technique). The SpO2 of 88% is below target (91–95% for <35 weeks). Increasing CPAP alone (A) without surfactant is insufficient for moderate-severe RDS at this gestation.
*Exam Pearl: Surfactant criteria — FiO2 >0.30 on CPAP ≥6 in <30 weeks with RDS = intubate + surfactant.*

---

**Q6 — B (Emergency needle decompression)**
Sudden haemodynamic collapse + absent breath sounds + contralateral mediastinal shift = tension pneumothorax. IMMEDIATE management = needle decompression (2nd ICS, MCL, affected side) before waiting for CXR or chest tube. Chest tube (C) is definitive but takes longer.
*Trap: Do not wait for CXR in tension pneumothorax — it is a clinical diagnosis.*

**Q14 — D (512 mL)**
Double-volume exchange = 2 × 85 mL/kg × weight = 2 × 85 × 3.2 = 544 ≈ nearest option is 512 mL (some centres use 80 mL/kg per body volume). Calculation: 2 × 80 × 3.2 = 512 mL.
*Exam Pearl: Double-volume exchange = 2 × 85 mL/kg (blood volume) = 170 mL/kg.*

**Q15 — B (Therapeutic hypothermia)**
Therapeutic hypothermia (33–34°C for 72 hours) started within 6 hours is the ONLY proven neuroprotective therapy for moderate-severe HIE in term neonates. Phenobarbitone (A) treats seizures but does not treat HIE itself and is not first-line before hypothermia.
*Exam Pearl: Hypothermia must start within 6 hours of birth and must be in a term/near-term (≥36 weeks) neonate.*

---

**Q19 — D (Oral glucose gel)**
For asymptomatic neonatal hypoglycaemia with ability to feed: oral glucose gel 200 mg/kg buccal + breastfeed is the evidence-based first step (Harding 2017 trials). IV bolus (A) is for symptomatic hypoglycaemia or inability to feed. The gel avoids supplementation with formula and supports breastfeeding.

---

**Q47 — B (Knee-chest + morphine + propranolol)**
Hypercyanotic ("Tet") spell management: knee-chest position (increases SVR), oxygen, morphine (reduces hyperpnoea, decreases RV outflow obstruction), IV propranolol (reduces infundibular spasm), IV phenylephrine (raises SVR). Adenosine (C) is for SVT, not Tet spells.

---

**Q67 — B (Ethosuximide)**
Childhood absence epilepsy — first-line is ethosuximide OR valproate. Ethosuximide is preferred when ONLY absence seizures are present (no generalized tonic-clonic seizures) because it avoids valproate's side effects. Carbamazepine (A) and oxcarbazepine (D) can WORSEN absence seizures.
*Trap: Carbamazepine is absolutely contraindicated in absence epilepsy — it worsens seizures.*

---

**Q69 — A (5 minutes)**
Operational definition of SE (ILAE 2015): ≥5 minutes of continuous seizure activity OR ≥2 seizures without return to baseline. The old definition of 30 minutes is outdated.

---

**Q85 — C (IV calcium gluconate)**
In hyperkalaemia with ECG changes (peaked T waves), the FIRST and most URGENT treatment is IV calcium gluconate — it stabilizes the cardiac membrane. It does NOT lower potassium but prevents fatal arrhythmia while other measures work. Then give salbutamol (A), NaHCO3 (B), and/or dialysis (D).
*Exam Pearl: ECG changes in hyperkalaemia → Calcium FIRST (membrane stabilizer). Then shift K into cells.*

---

**Q97 — D (Severe dehydration — RL 30 mL/kg then 70 mL/kg)**
Per WHO/IAP guidelines for severe dehydration: Ringer's lactate (preferred) 30 mL/kg over 30 minutes (infants) or 30 min, then 70 mL/kg over 2.5 hours. Option C (0.9% NaCl 20 mL/kg bolus) is for shock (PALS approach), not the WHO severe dehydration protocol for gastroenteritis.

---

**Q110 — C (Exchange transfusion + oxygen + antibiotics + analgesia)**
Acute chest syndrome with SpO2 <90% in SCD requires EXCHANGE transfusion (not simple top-up) to rapidly reduce HbS% to <30%. Simple transfusion (B) may cause hyperviscosity. Oxygen, IV antibiotics (broad-spectrum including atypicals), analgesia, and bronchodilators are adjuncts.
*Exam Pearl: ACS = medical emergency in SCD → Exchange transfusion target HbS <30%.*

---

**Q119 — B (10 mL/kg NS only if shock)**
Per ISPAD 2022 and BSPED 2020 DKA guidelines: Do NOT give routine fluid boluses in DKA unless there is haemodynamic compromise (shock). Boluses increase cerebral oedema risk. Start deficit replacement IV fluids + insulin at 0.1 U/kg/hr after 1 hour of fluid alone.
*Exam Pearl: DKA fluid bolus ONLY for shock. Routine boluses are no longer recommended (ISPAD 2022).*

---

**Q121 — C (Cerebral oedema)**
Cerebral oedema is the most feared complication of DKA in children (0.3–1%). Risk factors: age <5, high BUN, low PCO2, bicarbonate therapy, rapid fluid infusion. Symptoms: headache, behavioural change, declining GCS, bradycardia, fixed dilated pupil (herniation).
Treatment: Mannitol 0.5–1 g/kg IV over 20 min OR 3% NaCl 2.5–5 mL/kg, restrict fluids.

---

**Q132 — A (IVIG 2 g/kg + high-dose aspirin)**
Kawasaki disease treatment: IVIG 2 g/kg single infusion + high-dose aspirin 30–50 mg/kg/day (anti-inflammatory dose) during acute phase. Z-score 2.8 = coronary artery dilatation (abnormal >2.5). After fever resolves, switch to low-dose aspirin 3–5 mg/kg/day.

---

**Q149 — B (2 J/kg = 40 J)**
Paediatric defibrillation dose: 2 J/kg for first shock. Second shock: 4 J/kg. Subsequent shocks: 4 J/kg (max 10 J/kg or adult dose 360 J). For 20 kg child: first shock = 2 × 20 = 40 J.

---

**Q150 — B (Lung-protective ventilation)**
ARDS management: lung-protective ventilation (tidal volume 6 mL/kg ideal body weight, PEEP titration, plateau pressure <28 cmH2O). Permissive hypercapnia (pH >7.20 is acceptable) prevents ventilator-induced lung injury (VILI). HFOV (D) is a rescue therapy, not first-line in paediatric ARDS per PALICC guidelines.
